## Verdict reasoning

**Category (f) — real and correctly stated**, with two secondary details in the register needing
correction (below). Everything load-bearing checks out.

**1. The claim exists and is a significance claim.** `docs/modeling_results.md:56-63`, verbatim:

> **The method shows a small, statistically real, but practically negligible signal — and falls well
> short of being a usable rock-abundance predictor at this stage.** Across the 12 (variant × scale)
> regression configurations, every model has a presence-AUC above 0.5 (sign-test *p* = 0.0002) and ten
> of twelve have a positive Spearman ρ (sign-test *p* = 0.019); …

"statistically real" is cashed out by exactly two statistics, both sign tests, and nothing else in the
paragraph is a test (`shadow_fraction` importance is a plausibility argument). §2.1 then says at
`:174-180`:

> The fold-by-fold sign test is on the edge of conventional significance (*p* = 0.063), but the
> configuration-level tests are **decisive**: it is very unlikely that all twelve **independent**
> models would land above chance on AUC by random fluctuation alone.

**2. The producer is `scripts/probes/_summarize_modeling_results.py:66-78`** — `binomtest(n_pos, 12,
0.5, alternative="greater")` over the 12 rows of `aggregate.parquet`. All four published p-values and
both published means reproduce to 4 dp from `models/_sweep/20260524T071830Z`, so the attribution is
unambiguous.

**3. The 12 units are not 12 draws.** They are 12 genuinely distinct *conditions* (3 objectives × 4
nested scales) — so "re-analyses" is rhetorical — but they are all fit on the **same 8 LOIO folds of
the same 9 images**, and the sampling unit the binomial null needs to be exchangeable over is the
image, not the condition. Measured: the mean pairwise correlation of per-image values across the 12
configs is **0.566** (ρ) and **0.721** (AUC) → n_eff **1.7** and **1.3**, not 12.

**4. The decisive test — the test's actual size.** I flipped the sign of each image's deviation
exhaustively (2^8 = 256 assignments), recomputed the 12 config means and the published one-sided
binomial p each time. Under that image-level null:

| test | published p | exact image-clustered p | true rejection rate at nominal α = 0.05 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ρ > 0, 10/12 | 0.0193 | **0.324** | **0.324** |
| AUC > 0.5, 12/12 | 0.000244 | **0.180** | **0.371** |
| ρ > 0, 56/96 folds | 0.0627 | 0.281 | 0.219 |

The published test rejects a true null about **1 time in 3**. The p-values are anticonservative by
~17× (ρ) and ~740× (AUC). This is not a quibble about how to choose a unit — the stated error rate is
simply not the test's error rate.

**5. The conclusion does flip, in the half that matters.** At the LOIO image unit nothing is
significant by any test I tried: sign 4/8 p=0.64 and 5/8 p=0.36; Wilcoxon 0.32 / 0.23; one-sample t
0.198 / 0.142; exact sign-flip 0.246 / 0.164. Tested one config at a time (no averaging), **0 of 12**
configs reach p < 0.05 on either quantity. Single-image fragility is severe: dropping
`ESP_054857_2270` takes ρ from 10/12 (p=0.019) to **4/12 (p=0.93)**; dropping `ESP_056165_2200` takes
AUC from 12/12 (p=0.0002) to **7/12 (p=0.39)**. So the "small, **statistically real**" half of the
Bottom line is not supported by the evidence the Bottom line cites. The "practically negligible /
falls well short of usable" half is unaffected (it is the conservative direction).

## Corrected claim

The register is right; two supporting details should be fixed when it is actioned.

1. **Which image carries it.** The register says "one image (ESP_056165_2200, mean AUC 0.661) carries
   the result" immediately after listing the per-image mean **ρ**. In fact there are **two** distinct
   load-bearing images, one per headline: **ESP_054857_2270** (mean ρ +0.128) carries the ρ result
   (drop it → 4/12, p = 0.93), and **ESP_056165_2200** (mean AUC 0.661) carries the AUC result
   (drop it → 7/12, p = 0.39). This makes the finding *stronger*, not weaker.
2. **"the more-nearly-independent fold-level test (56/96, p = 0.063)".** Not accurate. The 96-cell test
   is 12 configs × the **same 8 images** — equally clustered (exact image-clustered p = 0.281; true
   size at nominal α = 0.05 is 0.219). Its larger p reflects the extra noise in single-fold ρ, not any
   correction for dependence. Reword to "the noisier fold-level test, which is clustered the same way".
3. **Add the strongest number, which the register omits:** the published 12-config test's *actual*
   type-I error rate under an image-level null is **0.32 (ρ) / 0.37 (AUC)** at nominal α = 0.05, and
   the exact image-clustered p-values for the published statistics are **0.324** and **0.180**. That
   is the like-for-like correction (it uses all 96 cells) and is more persuasive than the n = 8 sign
   test, which a defender could dismiss as underpowered.
