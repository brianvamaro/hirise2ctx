## Verdict reasoning

This is **(f) real and correctly stated** on mechanism, and every quoted number is exact.

The two-factor structure is unambiguous in the code. `run_threshold` retrains on the regenerated target
*and* stores that regenerated target as the truth column:

```python
# scripts/probes/_diag_tier2_minconf_sweep.py:100-110
for f in folds:
    ytr, m1 = remap(f.keys_train, lut, f.y_train["fractional_area"].to_numpy())
    yte, m2 = remap(f.keys_test,  lut, f.y_test["fractional_area"].to_numpy())
    ...
    mdl.fit(f.X_train[~vm], ytr[~vm], eval_set=(f.X_train[vm], ytr[vm]))
    d = f.keys_test[["obs_id", "ti", "tj"]].copy()
    d["y_true"] = yte                      # <-- the FILTERED target
    d["y_pred"] = mdl.predict(f.X_test)    # <-- model trained on the FILTERED target
```

and the paired statistic quoted in the record compares `ρ(pred_t, y_t)` against `ρ(pred_none, y_none)`:

```python
# scripts/probes/_diag_tier2_minconf_sweep.py:152-158
rho = per_img(df)
if label == "none":
    base_rho, ptxt = rho, "(baseline)"
else:
    keys = [k for k in base_rho if k in rho and ...]
    a = np.array([rho[k] for k in keys]); b = np.array([base_rho[k] for k in keys])
    ptxt = f"paired d={np.median(a-b):+.3f} wins {int((a>b).sum())}/{len(keys)} p={wilcoxon(a,b).pvalue:.3f}"
```

Two different predictors against two different ground truths, so a negative Δ cannot distinguish "the
model got worse" from "the estimand got harder". My decomposition puts essentially all of it in the
estimand: −0.0172 of the −0.0210 is the target factor, and the model factor is −0.0034 (p = 0.43).
Note the model factor is *conservatively signed* — `pred_none` has home-field advantage when scoring
against `y_none` — and it still comes out null. The heads are seeded (`seeds=(0,1,2)`,
`torch.manual_seed(seed)` at `_fm_tier2_regression.py:173-175`), so the null is not swamped by run-to-run
noise (though no same-target re-run exists to bound that noise floor).

**Two corrections to the register entry (why severity comes down to medium).**

1. **The "blocks R23's fix" framing overstates.** R23's remedy is to set `min_confidence` to the max of
   the per-image kept-score minima = **0.617** (register R23, "Fix"). That is above the arm where R56
   demonstrates a null (0.5) and adjacent to the arm where the honest one-factor test is borderline

   criterion for R23: harmonising the cohort *redefines the estimand*, so ρ against the new target and ρ

   quotes is no more (and no less) usable than the one-factor number R56 proposes. Harmonising at 0.617

   re-run of this ρ comparison.

## Corrected claim

The Stage-2b verdict "`min_confidence` filtering is HARMFUL — monotonically degrades both ranking and
dynamic range; low-confidence detections are real boulders, not removable noise" rests on a comparison in
which **both the model and the ground truth changed**. Decomposed on the banked parquets: the target
factor is −0.0172 (p = 0.0002) at conf ≥ 0.5 and −0.0557 at conf ≥ 0.7, while the **model** factor —
training on higher-confidence labels, scored against one common unfiltered target — is **−0.0034
(p = 0.43, 17/38 wins)** at 0.5 and −0.0213 (p = 0.061) at 0.7, with pooled ρ rising 0.6478 → 0.6506.
The dynamic-range half is likewise a target-redefinition artefact: at a matched population fraction
`top_ratio` goes 0.664 → 0.623 → 0.521 (not 0.664 → 0.578 → 0.314), and after the shipped LOIO
quantile-match against the common target it goes 0.870 → 0.860 → 0.829 (not 0.870 → 0.797 → 0.495).
The defensible verdict is "**not a lever** — no measurable ranking or level gain, and no measurable cost
at conf ≥ 0.5 either", not "harmful", and the mechanistic claim about BoulderNet's low-score detections
is not supported by this experiment at all. Separately, the sweep's premise of a uniform label basis is
false for 2 of its 38 images (`ESP_017355_2260` retention 1.000 = literally untreated; `ESP_068483_2280`
0.803) — an independent confirmation of R23 from a different artifact.
