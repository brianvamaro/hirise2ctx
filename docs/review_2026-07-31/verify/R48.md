**Note on the one discrepancy.** Every partial ρ matches to 4 dp; only the *p-value* of one boundary
cell differs. The area file used dof = n−2 (i.e. `pearsonr` on the residuals, not debiting the
covariate); the correct partial-correlation dof is n−3. On `dominant_source_frac_mean ↔ spearman_rho`
that is p = 0.049 vs **p = 0.0524**. So the register's "10 of 12 die" is reproduced *exactly* under its
own convention and is **conservative** — under the correct dof, and under the closed-form partial-Spearman
formula, **11 of 12 die and only one cell survives** (`mean_n_sources ↔ spearman_rho`, ρ_par = −0.343,
p = 0.038). This is a correction in the finding's favour, not against it.

**Three things I measured that the finding does not claim, all strengthening it:**

1. **The 12 raw cells do not survive any multiplicity correction, before partialling at all.** Over the
   20 tests, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at α = 0.05 rejects **0 of 20**, Bonferroni **0 of 20** (smallest
   p = 0.0116 vs a BH rank-1 threshold of 0.0025). BH over 20 correlated tests is harsh, but even
   correcting for the 4 features alone (min p × 4 = 0.046) leaves the whole grid marginal.
2. **On a genuinely base-rate-free instrument the grid is flat.** Re-running the same four features
   against **AP excess** (`pr_auc − meaningful_base_rate`; ρ with base rate = −0.031, p = 0.85) gives
   ρ = −0.082 / −0.123 / −0.184 / +0.199, **all p ≥ 0.23**. Same on `meaningful_auc` (the metric the
   project's own invariant 8 prescribes, and which the grid omitted in favour of `presence_auc`) once
   base rate is partialled out: **all four p ≥ 0.10**.
3. **Two of the three "independent" validating features are the same variable.**
   `Spearman(mean_n_sources, dominant_source_frac_mean) = −0.979`. Their ranges are also much narrower
   than the prose implies: `mean_n_sources` ∈ [1.000, 1.138] and `dominant_source_frac_mean` ∈
   [0.976, 1.000]. So the sentence "when a HiRISE footprint is stitched from **many** CTX sources the
   model performs worse" (`PROMOTION_QUEUE.md:153-156`, `docs/modeling.md:490-491`) is describing a
   feature whose per-tile mean source count never exceeds 1.14 — it is "the ≤14 % of tiles that
   straddle one seam", not the ~24–46 sources per footprint reported at `DECISIONS.md:1522`. The
   "three features all agree" framing is therefore ~2 quantities, not 3.

## Verdict reasoning

This is **(f) real and correctly stated** (with one number conservative in the register's direction).
I attacked it on five fronts and it survived all five.

**1. Is the verdict actually built on the grid, or on something else (category (a))?** On the grid. The
pre-declared criterion is unambiguous and appears in two places before the run:
`_diag_stage6b_h3_check.py:6-7` ("`rho(per_image_AUC, mean_CTX_INCIDENCE)` should be significantly
negative (rho < -0.30, p < 0.05)") and `_sweep_stage6b.py:19-24` (the same, as half of the Stage-6b
acceptance criterion). It failed on all three AUC-family metrics (+0.050 / −0.213 / −0.103, all
p > 0.19) and the probe reports the failure honestly at `_diag_stage6b_h3_check.md:9-11`. The 4×5 grid
is *not* pre-declared anywhere — it exists only inside `main()` at `:133-142`, alongside per-image
deltas, as a diagnostic print. The record then converts it into the verdict verbatim:
`PROMOTION_QUEUE.md:526-529` and `docs/modeling_results.md:1418-1423` both say "correlate negatively
(p < 0.05) with **every operational metric**". Four of the five metrics in that grid are the base rate
re-expressed at ρ = 0.92–0.98. So the verdict rests exactly where the finding says it rests.

**2. Is base rate a mediator rather than a confounder — i.e. is partialling it out wrong?** No. The
base rate is a property of the HiRISE BoulderNet labels and the terrain; it is causally upstream of,
and cannot be produced by, CTX mosaic seam structure or model behaviour. It is a confounder on both
arms (it drives the metric at ρ ≈ 0.98 and correlates with the CTX features at ρ = −0.37/−0.30/+0.34,
two of them at p < 0.05), so conditioning on it is the correct adjustment, not an over-adjustment.

**3. Is the confound already acknowledged (category (c))?** Not at the Stage-6b entries — I grepped
`DECISIONS.md`, `PROMOTION_QUEUE.md`, `docs/modeling*.md`, `ROADMAP.md`, `README.md`,
`PLAN_ModelUsability.md` for `base rate` / `base_rate` / `prevalence`. **One correction to the area
file**, which asserts the only mention is the §9.3 saturation caveat: `DECISIONS.md:1512-1516`
(notebook 13, 2026-05-29 — two days *before* Stage 6b) already records `bin_rich_base_rate ↔
bin_rich_auc = +0.36 (p = 0.027)` and comments "images with more boulder-rich content fit better —
partly trivial, partly informative". This does not rescue the verdict; it makes it worse. The project
had already seen the confound in a weaker form on a *different* metric, and then two days later built
the mechanism verdict on metrics where the same correlation is +0.98 without re-checking.

**4. Does the project's own `normalised_lift_at_top_k` already handle prevalence, as §3's "Rule to
adopt" (`CODE_REVIEW_2026-07-31.md:217-218`) asserts?** No — and Stage 6b *did* use it

base rates" in the next sentence. Measured: ρ = +0.9806 with base rate. So the register's own §3 rule
statement, and R26's exemption for this metric, are both wrong; the area file's sub-point is correct.

**5. Is the mechanism established independently, making the statistical defect moot?** Partly, and this

## Corrected claim

As stated, with two refinements:

1. **11 of 12, not 10 of 12** — only `mean_n_sources ↔ spearman_rho` (ρ_par = −0.343, p = 0.038)
   survives at the correct partial-correlation dof; the register's 10 is reproduced under its dof = n−2
   convention and is conservative.
2. The defect is not only the partial: **none of the 12 raw cells survives a multiplicity correction
   either** (BH-FDR 0/20, Bonferroni 0/20), and on two genuinely base-rate-free instruments — AP excess
   and base-rate-partialled `meaningful_auc` — **no feature reaches p < 0.05 at all**. What the Stage-6b
   data support is: *"images whose tiles more often straddle a CTX seam have a lower fraction of
   boulder-rich tiles, and after adjusting for that there is one marginal, uncorrected association
   between seam-straddling and per-image rank correlation."* Not a validated model-degradation
   mechanism.
